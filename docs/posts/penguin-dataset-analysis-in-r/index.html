Let’s Analyse Penguin Data with R

Author

Adnan Ali Syed

Published

September 27, 2026

Introduction

In this post, we will explore the Palmer Penguins dataset using R. We will compare the body mass and flipper length of the three penguin species.

The data comes from the Palmer Penguins dataset, collected by Palmer Station Antarctica LTER. The dataset is available directly through the palmerpenguins R package.

Load and clean the data

library(palmerpenguins)

penguins_clean <- na.omit(
  penguins[, c(
    "species",
    "island",
    "bill_length_mm",
    "flipper_length_mm",
    "body_mass_g"
  )]
)

head(penguins_clean)
  species    island bill_length_mm flipper_length_mm body_mass_g
1  Adelie Torgersen           39.1               181        3750
2  Adelie Torgersen           39.5               186        3800
3  Adelie Torgersen           40.3               195        3250
5  Adelie Torgersen           36.7               193        3450
6  Adelie Torgersen           39.3               190        3650
7  Adelie Torgersen           38.9               181        3625

I selected the variables needed for this analysis and removed rows containing missing values.

Calculate average measurements by species

species_summary <- aggregate(
  cbind(body_mass_g, flipper_length_mm) ~ species,
  data = penguins_clean,
  FUN = mean
)

species_summary
    species body_mass_g flipper_length_mm
1    Adelie    3700.662          189.9536
2 Chinstrap    3733.088          195.8235
3    Gentoo    5076.016          217.1870

The table shows that the three species differ in both average body mass and average flipper length.

Count the number of penguins in each species

species_counts <- table(penguins_clean$species)

species_counts

   Adelie Chinstrap    Gentoo 
      151        68       123

This tells us how many observations from each species remain after removing missing values.

Compare body mass visually

boxplot(
  body_mass_g ~ species,
  data = penguins_clean,
  xlab = "Species",
  ylab = "Body mass (g)"
)

The boxplot shows noticeable differences in body mass between the species. Gentoo penguins generally have higher body masses than Adelie and Chinstrap penguins.

Conclusion

The R analysis gives a similar result to the Python analysis. The penguin species have different physical characteristics, and Gentoo penguins are generally heavier and have longer flippers than the other two species.
